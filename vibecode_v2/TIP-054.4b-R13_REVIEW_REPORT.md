## Critical assessment

1. Security boundary is sound at design level: fresh active actor projection after
   acquisition, owner comparison before receipts, no source-content grants,
   no arbitrary externally supplied row-as-authority, unchanged receipt joins,
   fields/order/LIMIT 100, and pure DTO construction only after awaited cleanup.
   Actor authority is observed at its projection statement, not guaranteed active
   at response delivery; fusion must not be marketed as eliminating that existing
   TOCTOU window or giving latest-at-return authority.
2. READ COMMITTED is necessary for this stated statement-observation contract but
   is not proved by the current engine source. Unknown/custom/RR/serializable or
   mismatched policy/execution authority origins must route to legacy before DB
   work. Matching DSNs are not sufficient authority identity. The final addendum
   requirement correctly prevents a trusted-factory guess from becoming code.
3. Removing the old `_view` row observation changes permitted concurrent metadata
   observations. Cancel committed before the one row statement must be visible;
   after it may not be. Later receipts may reflect newer committed work. The final
   design explicitly rejects atomic row/receipt and latest-at-return guarantees,
   and requires compatibility acceptance or abandonment of the three-query target.
4. Query budget is honest and bounded. A continuous one-checkout hold can be longer
   than each old hold; neither the lower statement count nor the R12 observer
   difference proves improved contention or latency. Physical events, hold duration,
   all attempted/error/missing populations and supported sample sizes are required.
5. Commit/audit invariants remain immutable: `_owner` for cancel/resume/output and
   `_view` for submit/internal paths stay semantically intact; acknowledged commit
   plus final error is not rollback; no retry, replay or mutation fallback is added.
   B/C do not enter A by implication, and no broad RequestConnection rewrite is
   necessary to obtain design acceptance.
6. The eight future control groups cover the relevant risk surface without a new
   architecture. They remain planned, not executable inventory or runtime PASS.
   Future approval must freeze exact tests and candidate bytes, review premise/code
   before execution, stop on correctness/isolation/cleanup failure, and seek a
   separate bounded-load approval. Original thresholds and failed populations stay.
